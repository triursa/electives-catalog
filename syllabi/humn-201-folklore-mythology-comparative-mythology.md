# HUMN 201: Folklore, Mythology, and Comparative Mythology

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 111.82–137.82 h of required work

A comparative study of folklore and mythology across world cultures. Read creation myths, hero tales, trickster narratives, and folktales from Greek, Norse, Mesopotamian, Indian, Celtic, Native American, African, and Mesoamerican traditions. Apply structural, functionalist, Jungian, and diffusionist theories to understand how myths shape and reflect the cultures that produce them.

**All required materials are freely available online.** No purchase or paid subscription is needed.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 8.3 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Define myth, legend, folktale, and folklore, and distinguish among them using genre criteria from Bascom and Dundes.
- Identify recurring motifs and narrative structures across world mythologies using the Aarne-Thompson-Uther and Stith Thompson motif indexes.
- Apply major theoretical frameworks to myths, including functionalist (Malinowski), structuralist (Levi-Strauss), Jungian archetype (Campbell), ritualist (Frazer, Harrison), and morphological (Propp) approaches.
- Compare creation myths, hero journeys, trickster narratives, and apocalypse myths from at least five distinct cultural traditions.
- Analyze how myths encode social values, cosmological beliefs, and political structures within their source cultures.
- Evaluate the strengths and limitations of the comparative method in cross-cultural mythology studies.
- Construct an original comparative analysis of two myths from different traditions, supported by primary texts and secondary scholarship.
