# POLS 101: American Civics, The Federal Government

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 68.5–84 h of required work

An introduction to the United States federal government focusing on how the three branches interlock, their constitutional responsibilities, and the system of checks and balances that binds them. Read the Constitution and Federalist Papers alongside a modern textbook, trace power from founding theory to present-day practice, and develop a working understanding of how legislation becomes law, how the executive enforces it, and how the judiciary interprets it.

**All required materials are freely available online.** No purchase or paid subscription is needed.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 5.1 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Explain the constitutional design of separation of powers and checks and balances.
- Identify the specific powers granted to each branch by Articles I, II, and III.
- Trace the legislative process from bill introduction to presidential signature or veto override.
- Analyze the presidency as both head of state and head of government, including executive orders, pardons, and treaty power.
- Describe the federal court system from district courts to the Supreme Court and the role of judicial review.
- Evaluate how interbranch tensions, from impeachment to veto overrides to judicial confirmation, shape policy outcomes.
- Assess the role of the federal bureaucracy as the fourth branch and its relationship to all three constitutional branches.
- Apply constitutional reasoning to current events involving interbranch conflict.
