# LGST 101: Basics of Corporate Law

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 98.92–125.58 h of required work

An introductory survey of corporate law covering the legal environment of business, agency relationships, partnerships, corporate formation and governance, fiduciary duties, securities regulation, and contemporary debates about corporate purpose. Designed for students with no prior legal background.

**Some required materials require purchase or library access.** This course assigns books that are not freely available online. Check the task resource list for each week before starting.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 7.5 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Identify the sources of American law and the structure of the US legal system as it applies to business entities.
- Explain the principal-agent relationship and describe the duties and liabilities that arise from agency.
- Distinguish among sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited liability companies, and corporations.
- Describe the process of corporate formation, including filing requirements and the concept of separate legal personality.
- Analyze the doctrine of limited liability and the circumstances under which courts may pierce the corporate veil.
- Explain the fiduciary duties of corporate directors and officers, including duty of care, duty of loyalty, and the business judgment rule.
- Describe the basics of corporate finance, including stocks, bonds, dividends, and initial public offerings.
- Evaluate competing theories of corporate purpose, including shareholder primacy and stakeholder governance.
