# PHIL 101 midterm questions

Coverage: Weeks 1–7. This is an original self-assessment, not an accredited examination.

Create `04 Midterm/My Answers` in your external workspace. Allow **120 minutes**: about 50 minutes for short answers, 60 for the essay, and 10 to check your work. Work without notes. If you use an accommodation or a different time limit, record that choice for your own comparison. Save your attempt before opening the separate answer guide.

## Part A · Ten short answers · 60 points

Aim for 3–5 sentences per answer. Each question is worth 6 points: 2 for the central distinction or claim, 2 for explaining its reasoning, and 2 for a relevant example, objection, or implication.

1. What distinguishes a philosophical question from a question settled by a single observation? Give an example and explain why the distinction is not an absolute separation from empirical inquiry.
2. Distinguish a deductively valid argument from a sound argument. Give a valid argument with a false premise.
3. Explain why Socrates challenges a definition that gives only an example of piety in *Euthyphro*. What more is he seeking?
4. State the two directions of explanation in the Euthyphro dilemma. Why might agreement among the gods fail to settle the definitional question?
5. What is the purpose of Descartes' method of doubt? Explain the role of one skeptical scenario without assuming Descartes permanently accepts skepticism.
6. Explain the force of the cogito in the context of doubting. Identify something it does not yet establish by itself.
7. Distinguish determinism from fatalism and explain what a compatibilist claims about freedom and determinism.
8. Explain the traditional analysis of knowledge as justified true belief. Sketch why a lucky true belief can challenge a proposed account of knowledge.
9. Reconstruct Hume's problem of induction. Explain why pointing to induction's past success risks assuming what is in question.
10. Reconstruct one cosmological or design argument discussed in the course. Give one objection that targets a specific premise or inferential step.

## Part B · Choose one essay · 40 points

Write approximately 400–600 words. Identify your prompt. Use the assigned ideas accurately; exact quotations or page numbers are not required in this closed-note attempt.

### Prompt 1: What does doubt establish?

Evaluate one step in Descartes' movement from doubt toward knowledge in Meditations I–III. Reconstruct the step, present a substantive objection, consider a reply, and defend your assessment.

### Prompt 2: Can induction be justified?

Explain Hume's challenge and assess whether an appeal to custom solves it. Distinguish a psychological explanation of belief from a rational justification. Defend your conclusion against an objection.

### Prompt 3: Is divine approval enough?

Explain the Euthyphro dilemma and evaluate whether divine approval adequately defines piety or moral rightness. Distinguish the dialogue's question from a modern extension you introduce. Give an objection and a reply.

## Essay scoring

- Thesis and focus: 4 points.
- Accurate reconstruction: 12 points.
- Evaluation, objection, and reply: 12 points.
- Relevant use of assigned ideas: 8 points.
- Organization and clarity: 4 points.

After saving, mark the independent attempt complete. Use the Week 9 correction task and the separate answer guide to identify what to revisit. A score is a diagnostic for your learning, not a credential.
