# LGST 201: Basics of Criminal Law

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 85.5–124.75 h of required work

An introductory survey of American criminal law, covering the sources and structure of criminal law, constitutional protections, the elements of a crime, defenses, parties to crime, inchoate offenses, and major categories of criminal offenses including homicide, property crimes, and crimes against the public. Designed for undergraduates and self-study learners with no prior legal background.

**All required materials are freely available online.** No purchase or paid subscription is needed.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 7 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Identify the sources of American criminal law and distinguish criminal law from civil law.
- Explain the constitutional protections that govern the criminal justice system, including key Bill of Rights provisions.
- Analyze the elements of a crime: actus reus, mens rea, causation, and concurrence.
- Evaluate criminal defenses including self-defense, necessity, duress, insanity, and intoxication.
- Distinguish between principals, accomplices, and accessories in party liability.
- Classify inchoate offenses: attempt, conspiracy, and solicitation.
- Differentiate categories of criminal homicide: murder, manslaughter, and their degrees.
- Identify elements of property crimes, public order crimes, and crimes against the government.
- Apply criminal law concepts to hypothetical fact patterns and case analyses.
- Articulate theories of punishment and their implications for criminal justice policy.
