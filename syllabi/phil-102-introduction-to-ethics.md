# PHIL 102: Introduction to Ethics

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 116.25–141.25 h of required work

A question-driven introduction to ethical theory. Explore how philosophers have answered the question 'How should we live?' from ancient Greece to the present. Read primary texts by Aristotle, Kant, Mill, and others, reconstruct their arguments, test objections, and develop your own moral reasoning. Covers virtue ethics, deontology, utilitarianism, social contract theory, care ethics, and applied ethics.

**All required materials are freely available online.** No purchase or paid subscription is needed.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 8.6 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Distinguish metaethics, normative ethics, and applied ethics as levels of moral inquiry.
- Reconstruct the central arguments of major ethical theories including virtue ethics, deontology, utilitarianism, and social contract theory.
- Evaluate competing ethical theories using thought experiments, counterexamples, and real-world cases.
- Apply ethical frameworks to contemporary moral problems such as animal rights, environmental ethics, and bioethics.
- Articulate and defend your own position on a moral question in clear, structured prose with textual evidence.
- Identify the strengths and weaknesses of relativism, objectivism, and the relationship between religion and morality.
