# IDST 201: Systems Thinking

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 98.25–122.75 h of required work

A mixed-theory-and-practice introduction to systems thinking, the discipline of seeing wholes rather than parts. Drawn from engineering, ecology, management, and social science, the course covers feedback loops, stocks and flows, system archetypes, leverage points, and system dynamics modeling. Apply these tools to real-world problems from organizational design to sustainability.

**Some required materials require purchase or library access.** This course assigns books that are not freely available online. Check the task resource list for each week before starting.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 7.4 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Define a system and distinguish systems thinking from reductionist analysis.
- Identify stocks, flows, and feedback loops (balancing and reinforcing) in real-world systems.
- Construct causal loop diagrams to map system structure and behavior.
- Recognize and diagnose common system archetypes (success to the successful, shifting the burden, fixes that fail, etc.).
- Apply Donella Meadows's leverage points framework to identify high-leverage interventions.
- Build and simulate a basic system dynamics model using Vensim PLE.
- Evaluate systems thinking approaches in organizational, ecological, and social contexts.
- Synthesize systems thinking principles into a final analysis of a self-selected complex problem.
