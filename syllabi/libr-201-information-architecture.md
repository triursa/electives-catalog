# LIBR 201: Information Architecture

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 89.25–122 h of required work

An intermediate introduction to information architecture: the theory and practice of organizing digital information so it is findable, navigable, and understandable. Covers organization systems, labeling, navigation, search, metadata, controlled vocabularies, user research methods (card sorting, tree testing, content audits), IA strategy, and the ethical design of information environments. Includes a semester-long applied IA project on a real website or system.

**Some required materials require purchase or library access.** This course assigns books that are not freely available online. Check the task resource list for each week before starting.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 7 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Define information architecture and explain the problems it addresses in digital environments.
- Analyze and evaluate the organization, labeling, navigation, and search systems of an existing website.
- Apply user research methods including content audits, card sorting, and tree testing to inform IA decisions.
- Design and document an IA strategy including sitemaps, wireframes, and taxonomy structures.
- Understand the role of metadata, controlled vocabularies, and thesauri in supporting findability.
- Evaluate the ethical dimensions of information architecture and its impact on users and society.
- Produce a complete IA project from research through strategy to design documentation and presentation.
