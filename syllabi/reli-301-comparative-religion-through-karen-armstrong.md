# RELI 301: Comparative Religion Through the Work of Karen Armstrong

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 109.67–142.58 h of required work

A 15-week intermediate course on comparative religion structured around the major works of Karen Armstrong, one of the world's foremost scholars of religion. Beginning with her memoir The Spiral Staircase and her Short History of Myth, the course moves through her comparative histories of the monotheistic traditions (A History of God, Islam, Holy War), her Axial Age study (The Great Transformation) and biography of the Buddha, her analyses of modernity and violence (The Battle for God, Fields of Blood), and her reflections on scripture, compassion, and nature (The Lost Art of Scripture, The Case for God, Twelve Steps to a Compassionate Life, Sacred Nature). Students write two comparative essays and a final research paper.

**All required materials are freely available online.** No purchase or paid subscription is needed.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 8.4 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Trace Karen Armstrong's biographical journey and explain how her personal experience shapes her comparative method
- Define and apply the distinction between mythos and logos as Armstrong frames it
- Compare the development of monotheism across Judaism, Christianity, and Islam using Armstrong's historical framework
- Explain the Axial Age concept and evaluate its usefulness as a comparative tool across Chinese, Indian, Greek, and Israelite traditions
- Analyze Armstrong's argument that fundamentalism is a modern phenomenon rather than a return to premodern religion
- Evaluate Armstrong's claim that religion is not the primary cause of violence and that the secular state has been more destructive
- Distinguish premodern allegorical scriptural reading from modern literalism and explain why Armstrong considers literalism a distortion
- Articulate Armstrong's vision of compassion as the essence of religion and assess it critically
- Synthesize Armstrong's major themes into a coherent understanding of comparative religion as a discipline
