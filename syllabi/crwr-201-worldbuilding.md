# CRWR 201: Worldbuilding

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 109–134 h of required work

A mixed theory and practice course on constructing imaginary worlds for fiction, games, and transmedia. Study the history and theory of subcreation from Tolkien to modern franchise worldbuilding, then build your own world through a semester-long portfolio project. Read foundational texts, watch craft lectures, and produce worldbuilding documents weekly.

**Some required materials require purchase or library access.** This course assigns books that are not freely available online. Check the task resource list for each week before starting.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 8.1 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Define worldbuilding as a creative practice and distinguish it from setting, plot, and character.
- Analyze existing imaginary worlds across literature, film, games, and other media using critical frameworks.
- Construct a coherent fictional world with internal consistency across geography, history, culture, politics, and belief systems.
- Design magic systems or technological frameworks that serve narrative rather than undermine it.
- Apply techniques for revealing world information through scene and action rather than exposition dumps.
- Evaluate the ethical and cultural implications of worldbuilding choices, including representation and appropriation.
- Produce a comprehensive worldbuilding portfolio ready for use in a novel, game, or other creative project.
