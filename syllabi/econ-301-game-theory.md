# ECON 301: Game Theory and Strategic Analysis

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 116.5–146.5 h of required work

An intermediate introduction to game theory covering static and dynamic games with complete and incomplete information. Learn to model strategic interactions, compute Nash equilibria, analyze sequential games with backward induction, and apply game-theoretic reasoning to economics, politics, and everyday life. Combines formal theory with real-world applications.

**Some required materials require purchase or library access.** This course assigns books that are not freely available online. Check the task resource list for each week before starting.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 8.8 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Model strategic situations using normal form and extensive form game representations.
- Identify and compute Nash equilibria in pure and mixed strategies.
- Apply backward induction and subgame perfect equilibrium to sequential games.
- Analyze games of incomplete information using Bayesian Nash equilibrium and perfect Bayesian equilibrium.
- Evaluate repeated games, bargaining problems, and auction mechanisms.
- Apply cooperative game theory concepts including the Shapley value and the core.
- Critically assess the assumptions of rationality and common knowledge in game-theoretic models.
