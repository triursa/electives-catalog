# IDST 350: Tabletop RPG Design

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 114.58–143.17 h of required work

A mixed-theory-and-practice course in tabletop roleplaying game design. Study game design theory from Schell, Salen & Zimmerman, Koster, and Fullerton, apply it to the TTRPG medium through close play and deconstruction of existing games, and build a complete mini-RPG through iterative prototyping and playtesting. Covers resolution mechanics, character systems, conflict design, scenario structure, worldbuilding, narrative mechanics, and indie publishing.

**Some required materials require purchase or library access.** This course assigns books that are not freely available online. Check the task resource list for each week before starting.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 8.6 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Apply game design theory (Schell's lenses, Rules/Play/Culture schema, Koster's theory of fun) to the specific medium of tabletop RPGs.
- Analyze and deconstruct existing TTRPG resolution systems, character mechanics, and conflict models.
- Design and iterate a core resolution mechanic with documented probability distributions.
- Build a character creation system with meaningful progression choices.
- Design conflict resolution rules that create tension without halting narrative momentum.
- Structure scenarios using node-based design and revelation lists.
- Create setting documents and adventure modules that are immediately playable.
- Produce a complete, playtested mini-RPG (4-8 pages) with a design diary.
