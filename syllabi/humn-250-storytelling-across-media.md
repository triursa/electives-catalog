# HUMN 250: Storytelling Across Media

Version 1 · 15 weeks · 114.25–141.25 h of required work

A cross-media exploration of narrative craft spanning oral tradition, literature, film, television, and interactive games. Students study the cognitive and evolutionary foundations of storytelling, examine how structure, character, and theme operate differently across media, and produce original narrative work in multiple forms. The course treats theory and practice as inseparable: every concept is applied through writing, analysis, and creative projects.

**Some required materials require purchase or library access.** This course assigns books that are not freely available online. Check the task resource list for each week before starting.

This is an independent study plan. It does not award academic credit or a credential. The workload averages 8.5 hours per week; use the weekly ranges to plan your time. Stretch a week when a text needs more attention. Optional work is excluded from those totals.

## What you will learn

- Analyze the cognitive, evolutionary, and cultural functions of storytelling across human history.
- Identify and apply core narrative structures including three-act form, the hero's journey, and non-linear architectures.
- Construct compelling characters with internal conflict, desire, and dimensional depth.
- Compare how narrative techniques translate across oral, written, visual, and interactive media.
- Evaluate the role of audience agency and participation in interactive storytelling forms.
- Apply principles of pacing, tension, and dramatic irony to original narrative work.
- Revise and polish a narrative portfolio demonstrating craft across at least two media forms.
- Articulate a personal theory of story grounded in both classical theory and contemporary practice.
